KITE: Knowledge guided neural network with design code models and laboratory reference transfer for predicting long term creep and shrinkage of concrete from short term tests

1Kennesaw State University 2The Hong Kong Polytechnic University

Abstract

Qualifying a new low carbon concrete for long term deformation takes tests of months to years, and design code updating from a few weeks of data leaves large errors. KITE, a knowledge guided neural network, predicts the complete shrinkage or creep curve from the first 7 to 28 days of a test. An in source transfer module attends over the curves of a control mixture of the same program. An interpretable evidence gate weights the transferred trajectory against design code experts, an analog expert and a cross fitted tabular foundation model at every horizon, and conformal intervals quantify the uncertainty.

Method

Knowledge experts. ACI 209R-92, fib Model Code 2010 and Model B4 updated on the short term data, with MC2010 and B4 calibrated on the control mixture.

Analog expert. Attention over measured curves of other laboratories with similar short term kinetics.

Foundation model expert. TabPFN v2, cross fitted by research team, so every prediction seen by the gate comes from other teams.

M1. In source transfer. Attention over the curves of the control mixture, with a learned bias for the composition difference.

M2. Evidence gate. Weights for every expert at every horizon, and a residual bounded by the disagreement of the experts.
